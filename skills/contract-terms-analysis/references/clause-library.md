# Clause library by contract category

Vocabulary for sorting clauses by function within each contract category. Read the section matching the document; read more than one when a document spans categories.

## Consumer terms of service / EULA

- **Core deal** — what the service does, what the user is licensed to do (often "revocable, non-exclusive, non-transferable license," not ownership).
- **Rights-affecting** — mandatory arbitration, class action waiver, jury trial waiver, limitation of liability (often capped at what the user paid in the last 12 months, sometimes $0 for free services), IP assignment over user-generated content, unilateral modification rights ("we may change these terms at any time").
- **Financial exposure** — auto-renewal, cancellation mechanics (a surprising number require a phone call or physical mail during a narrow window), price-change notice period.
- **Boilerplate** — severability, entire agreement, governing law/venue (almost always the company's home jurisdiction), assignment (the company can transfer the contract; the user usually cannot), force majeure.

## Residential lease

- **Core deal** — rent, term, security deposit amount and conditions for return, permitted use and occupants.
- **Rights-affecting** — right of entry notice period, subletting/assignment restrictions, joint-and-several liability for co-tenants, acceleration clauses (unpaid rent becomes immediately due in full), waiver of jury trial, attorney's fees shifting (loser pays, or sometimes landlord-only).
- **Financial exposure** — late fees (some jurisdictions cap these), early termination fees, automatic renewal into month-to-month or a new fixed term, utility and maintenance cost allocation, pet deposits/rent.
- **Boilerplate** — notices clause, severability, entire agreement (merges any verbal promises from showing/negotiation into the written lease), governing law (usually the property's jurisdiction by default, less negotiable than in other contract types).

## Subscription / membership agreement

- **Core deal** — service tier, billing cycle, what triggers a tier or price change.
- **Rights-affecting** — arbitration and class waiver (often incorporated by reference to a separate ToS), data retention/deletion on cancellation, IP or account-lockout terms.
- **Financial exposure** — auto-renewal (the clause to check first: notice period required to avoid it, whether it converts monthly-to-annual), cancellation friction (self-service vs. requires contact), refund/proration policy, price-lock guarantees and their expiration.
- **Boilerplate** — the rest is usually a cross-reference to the parent company's general ToS and privacy policy rather than standalone language.

## Warranty

- **Core deal** — what's covered, what's excluded, duration, who performs the repair.
- **Rights-affecting** — whether it disclaims implied warranties (merchantability, fitness for purpose) to the extent state law allows, arbitration for warranty disputes, whether using third-party parts or service voids coverage (in the US, the Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act restricts tying a warranty's validity to using only the manufacturer's branded parts/service for routine maintenance, though this is a common overreach in warranty language anyway).
- **Financial exposure** — deductibles or shipping costs the owner bears, prorated vs. full replacement value as the product ages, what "labor" vs. "parts" coverage actually excludes.
- **Boilerplate** — registration requirements (a warranty is generally still valid without registering it, though registration often triggers the retailer's own reminder/support flow), the claims process itself.

## Employment offer / employment agreement

- **Core deal** — compensation structure, at-will status (in the US, most employment is at-will regardless of what the offer letter emphasizes), start date, reporting line.
- **Rights-affecting** — non-compete (unenforceable in some states entirely, e.g. California; narrowly enforceable in most others), non-solicitation of clients or employees, confidentiality and invention/IP assignment (watch for language sweeping in work done outside the job on personal time or equipment, which some states restrict), arbitration of employment disputes, non-disparagement.
- **Financial exposure** — clawback provisions on signing bonuses or relocation if the employee leaves within a window, commission/equity vesting cliffs, severance conditions.
- **Boilerplate** — at-will disclaimer, entire-agreement clause (supersedes verbal promises made during recruiting), policy-incorporation-by-reference to the employee handbook.

## Service agreement / freelance or vendor contract

- **Core deal** — scope of work, deliverables, payment schedule and milestones.
- **Rights-affecting** — IP ownership of work product (default rules differ: employees' work product usually belongs to the employer automatically, independent contractors' work product often does not unless the contract assigns it), indemnification (who bears the cost if the deliverable causes a third-party claim), liability caps, termination-for-convenience rights (can one side walk away without cause, and what's owed if they do).
- **Financial exposure** — late payment penalties or interest, kill fees, expense reimbursement terms, payment timing (net-30 vs. net-90 is a real cash-flow difference for a small vendor).
- **Boilerplate** — independent contractor status disclaimer, notices, force majeure, governing law.
